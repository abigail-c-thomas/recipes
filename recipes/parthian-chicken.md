The Parthian empire, which ruled over modern Iran and Iraq from the 2nd century BC until the 3rd century AD, was Rome's great eastern rival. While this dish may be named after Parthia, it probably is not Parthian in origin. The recipe itself is similar to many others found in [_Apicius_](/sources/apicius.html), and in particular both lovage and caraway are distinctly Roman ingredients. Instead, the dish likely gets its name due to its prominent use of [asafoetida](/ingredients/asafoetida.html), known to the Romans as Parthian _laser_ and used as a replacement for extinct _silphium_, or Cyrenaican _laser_. 

{% capture ingredients %}
* 1 whole chicken
* 300 ml white wine
* 45 ml [fish sauce](/ingredients/garum.html)
* 1.5 tsp dried lovage
* 1.5 tsp caraway seed
* 0.5 tsp [asafoetida](/ingredients/asafoetida.html)
* Black pepper
{% endcapture %}

{% capture instructions %}
1. Preheat the oven to 180°C / 350 F.
2. Quarter the chicken by first removing the backbone by cutting with a sharp knife or kitchen shears, then separate the breasts from the thighs and legs. Arrange the chicken pieces.
3. Dry-fry the caraway seeds, then grind in a mortar and pestle. Combine with wine, [fish sauce](/ingredients/garum.html), lovage, and [asafoetida](/ingredients/asafoetida.html), along with a generous amount of ground black pepper.
4. Place the chicken in a casserole dish, skin side up. Pour the sauce over the chicken, then place in the oven uncovered and bake for 50 minutes, or until the chicken is cooked through.
5. Once the chicken is cooked, remove from the oven, sprinkle with extra black pepper, and serve.
{% endcapture %}

{% capture notes %}
* Using bone-in, skin-on, chicken thighs can be a convenient alternative to quartering a whole chicken. Substitute 6 chicken thighs for a whole chicken.
* [Apicius](/sources/apicius.html) 6.8.6, for chicken in [_laser_](/ingredients/asafoetida.html) sauce, is an almost identical recipe but lacks the caraway seeds used here; so feel free to omit them if they are not to your taste.
{% endcapture %}
